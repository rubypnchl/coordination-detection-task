------------------------------------------------------------

Please help me break down the problem in simple terms:
- What is the actual objective?
- What is the difference between coordinated behaviour and an organic crowd?
- What fields in the data are likely to be useful?
- What simple approaches might fail?
- How should I think about validation when there are no labels?

Important: do not give me only a generic clustering answer. The task warns that hashtags, viral pile-ons, and embedding clustering can find crowds rather than real coordination.

What I took from this
------------------------------------------------------------

I decided to look for multiple signals together: shared target, multiple accounts, semantic similarity, compressed timing, and crowd penalties.

------------------------------------------------------------
Three moments that mattered
------------------------------------------------------------

1. Avoiding simple hashtag or embedding clustering

The first obvious idea was to cluster by hashtags or embeddings. I rejected this because those methods mostly find topics or crowds. The task specifically warns that viral hashtags and breaking-news pile-ons can look coordinated. This changed the method toward multi-signal scoring.

2. Treating candidate groups as candidates, not final findings

At first it was tempting to interpret shared keys as coordination. I changed this by separating candidate generation from scoring. A shared URL, hashtag, mention, reply, or thread only creates a candidate group. The final score depends on timing, semantic similarity, account diversity, and crowd penalties.

3. Automating evidence but keeping judgement human-in-the-loop

Manual review of every group was too slow. I automated content similarity, timing pattern, and target specificity, but did not treat the automatic label as ground truth. This gave a practical workflow: Python prepared evidence, and I used manual inspection to calibrate the logic and check failure modes.

------------------------------------------------------------
Final note on AI use
------------------------------------------------------------

AI was useful for scaffolding code, structuring the reasoning, and identifying possible failure modes. I used it as a coding and review assistant, not as an oracle. The main methodological choices came from the task constraints and from inspecting candidate groups in the dev split.
